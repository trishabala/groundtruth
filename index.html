The design constraint

The name is the specification

Groundtruth is borrowed from machine learning — the verified, ground-level fact a model is trained against, not a prediction and not a vibe. That's the bar for every number this product shows.

A palette can say the same thing before a word of copy loads. Forest, brass, and sage read as prepared, not alarmed — closer to a trail map than a crime map.

LAW 01

Sourced, not guessed

Every score traces to a named public dataset with an update timestamp. If there's no source, there's no score — just an honest "no data yet."

LAW 02

Calm, not alarmed

Ochre and sage, never red. Context — "more caution after 9pm" — never a verdict like "dangerous."

LAW 03

Useful before urgent

Value lands in Quick Check and Trip Mode, before you're already somewhere uncomfortable — not a post-incident report.

LAW 04

Honest about its limits

"No data yet" is a real, visible state. Gaps in coverage aren't filled with a plausible-looking number.

Why the data compounds

The loop nobody else is running

A crime-map overlay is copyable in a quarter. Millions of location queries, each timestamped and joined to whether the trip actually got booked, are not.

Quick Check query→ mapped to coverage gap→ prioritized in next city's data pull→ richer Trip Mode, next trip more usage → more coverage → wider lead

The free tier isn't just acquisition — every unanswered "no data yet" is a labelled demand signal for which city to onboard next.

What you'd actually own

Three hard problems, ranked

01 · hardest

Schema normalization at the edge of chaos

Thousands of city and county open-data portals, each with a different schema, update cadence, and reporting bias. Turning that into one consistent geospatial signal — without importing the uneven reporting rates as if they were uneven actual risk — is the real moat, not the UI.

02

Calibrating "context" instead of a score

The qualitative labels have to hold across cities with wildly different baseline rates, without either scaring people out of visiting a normal place or falsely reassuring them about a genuinely rough stretch of road.

03

Cheap, live ETA-to-risk matching

Matching a moving route to hourly risk windows, per stopover, on a multi-day trip — without a live API call on every keystroke. This is the same cost-vs-freshness tradeoff every real-time product eventually has to solve.

Before you get excited

What I don't know yet

Ranked by how badly it would hurt if it's wrong.

  • Whether "no data yet" costs more retention than a rough approximate score would. The honesty in Law 04 is a bet that trust compounds faster than coverage gaps repel people. Unmeasured.
  • Whether affiliate revenue funds the free tier, or B2B licensing has to carry it. Booking-platform commissions are real but thin; the model may need fleets, insurers, or corporate travel from day one, not later.
  • Whether people check before departure or only reactively mid-trip. Changes whether Quick Check or Trip Mode is the true front door — and which one deserves the marketing budget.
  • Whether Share My Trip reads as safety companion or surveillance to the exact audience it's meant to protect. The framing in the paywall matters more than the feature.
  • Whether open-data coverage is uneven enough that early users hit "no data yet" too often to stick around before the loop in the section above has time to work.
The ask

Feedback, or a technical sounding board

An interactive prototype with mock data, a naming decision, and a palette that says "prepared" instead of "afraid." Real infrastructure — Supabase/PostGIS, live routing, the actual crime-data ingestion pipeline — is the next phase, not this one. If you've built anything in this space, or can poke a hole in the coverage-gap assumption above, I'd rather hear it now.
